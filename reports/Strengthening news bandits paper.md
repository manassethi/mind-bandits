# Pre-empting reviewers: a four-week plan for the news-bandits paper

The paper's novelty holds up, but every headline result has a counterargument in the 2019–2026 literature, and that counterargument is what reviewers will raise. None of the five research notes found prior work that benchmarks power-sized A/B tests against bandits on MIND, that compares frozen decoder-LLM layers, Gemma Scope SAE features and sentence embeddings as bandit contexts under article churn, or that uses a policy-level faithfulness metric like "expected clicks lost". The weak points are specific. **First, the ~9x A/B-vs-TS gap compares against a power-sized test, which Test & Roll already showed to be the wrong size** ([Feit & Berman 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00457)). **Second, the SAE penalty of 22–35% matches a 2025–26 consensus that SAEs lose to dense baselines and barely beat random ones** ([Kantamneni et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16681); [Korznikov et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14111v1)). **Third, the LLM agent's 67–70% stuck rate is in line with Krishnamurthy et al.'s >60% failure at T=500, which makes it a replication, not a discovery** ([arXiv:2403.15371](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.15371v3)). **Fourth, every number comes from one learned simulator, and MIND's shuffled, propensity-free logs rule out off-policy evaluation** ([MIND docs](https://github.com/msnews/msnews.github.io/blob/master/assets/doc/introduction.md)). The highest-return fixes are cheap. Most are re-analyses of existing logs or CPU-only bandit runs: decision-quality metrics and a stronger A/B ladder for Q1, a misspecification diagnostic and random-SAE baselines for Q2, deletion curves with paired statistics for Q3, an LLM-prior Thompson sampling sweep and a tool-argmax baseline for Q4, and a simulator-family rank-stability table across all four questions. The four notes' wish lists add up to about 12 person-weeks. The plan below cuts them to roughly 15 working days of experiments plus a writing week before the November 20 CALE-AI deadline. Real-data replay, alternative-encoder simulators, slates and fine-tuning move to the 2027 venues.

## Reviewers will attack four soft spots, ranked by how likely they are to sink the paper

The notes converge on one ranked list of threats. The most dangerous is the A/B baseline. Feit & Berman show that profit-maximizing test sizes are "substantially smaller than typical hypothesis-test recommendations" and that a properly sized test & roll achieves "nearly the same expected regret as the multi-armed bandit" ([arXiv:1811.00457](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00457)). A reviewer who knows this paper will read the ~9x headline as a sizing artifact unless the paper breaks the gap into a sizing component and a non-adaptivity component. The same applies to "A/B never finishes at K=50". Elimination designs (Successive Elimination, Sequential Halving) exist to fix that failure, and Spotify chose group-sequential tests over always-valid methods because batched data gave them higher power ([Spotify Engineering](https://engineering.atspotify.com/2023/3/choosing-sequential-testing-framework-comparisons-and-discussions)).

The second threat is simulator dependence. The simulator reaches tiers 1–3 of the validation ladder in the simulator note: held-out fit, marginal statistics, and correlation with article-level CTR. It lacks tier 4, evidence that *policy rankings* survive a change of assumptions. The notes found no recsys paper that reports rank stability across simulators as standard. Accordion is the rare simulator judged on whether it reproduces decision-relevant differences ([McInerney et al., RecSys 2021](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1145%2F3460231.3474259)). Simulation-critique work faults the field for assumptions nobody examines and for missing sensitivity analysis ([Winecoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.14333)). That gap is a weakness, but it is also a cheap contribution to claim.

The third threat is that the SAE and LLM-agent results will read as expected rather than informative. SAE probes failed to beat baseline-only ensembles across four regimes ([Kantamneni et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16681)). Random SAE baselines nearly match trained SAEs on interpretability (0.87 vs 0.90), sparse probing (0.69 vs 0.72) and causal editing (0.73 vs 0.72) ([Korznikov et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14111v1)). LLM agents are known to be greedy: Gemma2-27B computes correct UCB rationales 87% of the time but still picks the greedy action 58% of the time ([Schmied et al., ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16078v1)). Without random-SAE controls and a "does the LLM add anything over its own tool?" baseline, reviewers can dismiss both results as known.

The fourth threat is a set of smaller credibility gaps a careful reviewer will list: the simulator's pooled AUC of 0.733 sits above the MIND leaderboard's best entry; the tuned UCB1 c=0.1 win at low CTR may be tuning leakage; the drift baselines are thin (discounted TS only); there are no batched updates; the "deeper layers are better" wording conflicts with a large mid-layer literature; and no layer-quality mechanism is offered. Each has a cheap fix, covered below.

## Q1: the A/B gap must survive Test & Roll, sequential stopping and elimination

The closest prior work is Test & Roll ([Feit & Berman, Marketing Science 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00457)). The theory explaining why fixed-length explore-then-commit still loses is Garivier, Lattimore & Kaufmann (NeurIPS 2016, arXiv:1605.08988; recorded from memory, not re-verified). They show that optimally tuned ETC is asymptotically suboptimal by about a factor of 2 relative to fully sequential policies on two Gaussian arms. That gives the paper a testable prediction: once A/B sizing is optimized, roughly 2x of the ~9x gap should remain, and that remainder is the cost of not adapting. The recent industry literature frames experimentation as a multi-objective problem. Amazon says adaptive-design guarantees are "poorly understood" outside stationary conditions and targets drift robustness and opportunity cost together ([Fiez et al., WWW 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10870)). eBay shows that naive TS "can falsely identify a single best arm" when arms tie, and proposes batched Top-Two TS as a replacement for fixed-horizon A/B ([Zhang & Yuan 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14704)). Simchi-Levi & Wang formalize a Pareto frontier between regret and treatment-effect estimation error ([AISTATS 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/simchi-levi23a.html); [Management Science 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v71y2025i6p4828-4846.html)), and the Mixture Adaptive Design gives anytime-valid inference on top of any bandit at small regret cost ([Liang & Bojinov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05794)). A paper that reports only cumulative clicks will look dated next to these.

The experiments follow directly from this. The paper needs a baseline ladder that runs from power-sized A/B through ETC-optimal (already present), Test & Roll sizing with a prior taken from MIND CTRs, O'Brien–Fleming group-sequential with 5–10 looks, and mSPRT or confidence-sequence stopping ([Johari et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04922); [Adobe's deployed confidence sequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10108)), to Successive Elimination and Sequential Halving for K>2. On the bandit side it needs Top-Two TS. Every method should report four numbers: in-experiment regret, Test & Roll-style total regret over a population N (test loss plus rollout loss), probability of correct selection (PCS) and simple regret of the arm deployed at T, and, as a stretch, bias and 95% CI coverage of the estimated lift. The existing "A/B BAI accuracy is only 60–80%" sentence is unanswerable without the matching TS and TTTS PCS numbers. Three cheap checks protect the tuned UCB1 result: KL-UCB, TS with an informative Beta prior at the base CTR (Beta(1,1) over-explores at ~3% CTR), and tuning c on held-out seeds. Kuleshov & Precup's empirical study is the precedent that tuned heuristics win but that tuning on the test condition flatters them (arXiv:1402.6028; unverified).

On drift, discounted TS alone is too thin. Reviewers will expect SW-UCB and D-UCB (Garivier & Moulines, ALT 2011; unverified) plus at least one change-detection restart method such as M-UCB, which was evaluated on Yahoo! news data ([Cao et al., AISTATS 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.03692)), or GLR-klUCB. All of these are available in [SMPyBandits](https://smpybandits.readthedocs.io/en/stable/NonStationaryBandits.html). The more important addition is **A/B with periodic re-testing**. The current "A/B and ε-greedy lock in" result holds by construction, and only a refreshed A/B losing is a realistic practice comparison. MIND has already been replayed in timestamp order for non-stationary bandits ([arXiv:2310.07786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07786)), so cite it as prior MIND drift work. Finally, a batch-size sweep (B ∈ {1, 10², 10³, 10⁴}) is now expected for any practical-experimentation claim. Chapelle & Li tested delayed updates on Yahoo! news ([NeurIPS 2011](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2011/hash/e53a0a2978c28872a4505bdb51db06dc-Abstract.html)), batched TS has strong guarantees ([Kalkanli & Özgür, NeurIPS 2021](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2021/poster/27500)), and a 2025 news-headline bandit paper simulates a 7-day delay ([arXiv:2506.06334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06334)). Batching does not affect the A/B test and penalizes deterministic UCB more than randomized TS, so it is also the most likely way for the UCB-beats-TS claim to break. Run it before that claim goes in the paper.

## Q2: frozen-LLM features are a NeuralLinear design, and misspecification explains the category failure

Most of the reframing work for Q2 is positioning. A frozen encoder with an exact Bayesian linear head is a "deep representation, shallow exploration" design. Neural-LinUCB explores only in the last linear layer and argues this is far cheaper than full-network exploration ([Xu et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.01780)). Deep Bayesian Bandits Showdown found that many approximate posteriors that work in supervised learning fail in sequential decisions ([Riquelme et al., ICLR 2018](https://research.google/pubs/deep-bayesian-bandits-showdown/)). The notes recall, without re-verifying, that NeuralLinear was among the most robust methods in that study. Citing both lets the paper present its design as "NeuralLinear with a foundation-model body" rather than as a naive baseline. LLMs in recent bandit work almost always serve as prior or pseudo-reward generators: CBLI ([EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1107/)), LOLA ([Ye et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02611v3)), calibration-gated pseudo-observations on MIND-small ([Pershin et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14961)) and LLM-derived Beta priors ([Lee et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03382)). The Pershin paper is a useful foil. It uses a single seed and T=100, and reports regret of 32 without the LLM vs 26 with it, so a multi-seed MIND-large study clears a far higher bar. Mortal bandits (Chakrabarti et al., NeurIPS 2008; unverified) are the classical framing for article lifetimes and should be cited for the churn manipulation.

The "category one-hot is worse than no features" result needs a mechanism, and theory supplies one. Under ε-misspecification, linear bandits pay an unavoidable ε·√d·T term ([Foster et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/84c230a5b1bc3495046ef916957c7238-Abstract.html); lower bound from Lattimore, Szepesvári & Weisz, summarized in [arXiv:2107.05745](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.05745)). Per-article Beta TS never pays that term. Because the simulator is known, ε can be measured directly: fit the best linear (and logistic) model of the simulator's true click probabilities on [1, a, u⊗a] for every feature set, then plot regret against ε. This takes one to two CPU days and turns an anecdote into a mechanism. Hybrid LinUCB, which adds per-article parameters to the shared model, is the canonical news baseline from Li et al. 2010 and directly tests whether per-article residuals rescue coarse features at slow churn but not fast churn. Rewards are low-CTR Bernoulli, which is exactly the regime where the logistic curvature constant hurts linear models ([Faury et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07530) is the relevant improvement; id unverified). A logistic-TS row (Laplace, full and diagonal) answers the "why linear?" question.

The layer result needs careful wording. The dominant recent finding is that intermediate layers often beat the final layer, both for general embeddings ([Skean et al., ICML 2025 oral](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/skean25a.html)) and for recommendation specifically. MARC reports that "representations from middle layers of LLMs outperform those from final layers in recommendation tasks" ([SIGIR 2026, arXiv:2604.18146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18146)). The paper should plot regret against *relative* depth for Gemma-3-1B and 12B on one axis and look explicitly for a dip in the last one or two layers, which MARC's next-token-specialization account predicts. It should say "later-middle layers" if the peak falls before the final layer, and present any monotone rise as a contrast with that literature rather than as a general rule. Adding a per-layer offline probe AUC and correlating it with online regret yields a cheap layer-selection rule. A random Gaussian projection at the same d separates "PCA picks good directions" from "the LLM space is good".

## Q3: SAE features must beat random SAEs before their labels mean anything

The SAE result (22–35% more regret than same-d PCA, but better than no features) matches the 2025–26 consensus. The framing should be an independent confirmation in a new setting: every negative result the notes found is for static probing, and none is for sequential decisions. Google DeepMind's own team reported dense probes at ~0.999 AUROC beating k-sparse SAE probes OOD, and found that probes on SAE reconstructions lose information ([Smith et al., Alignment Forum 2025](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/4uXCAJNuPKtKBsi28/negative-results-for-saes-on-downstream-tasks)). SAEs trained on *randomly initialized* transformers score similarly on auto-interp ([Heap et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17727)). The single most important addition is therefore a **Korznikov-style random-SAE baseline**: random encoder directions with JumpReLU thresholds calibrated to L0 ≈ 60, using the same pooling and selection, run through LinUCB. It needs no training. If trained SAEs beat it, the claim becomes "SAEs cost regret but carry real structure that buys auditability". If they don't, the paper must say so.

There is a confound to address. Unsupervised top-variance selection of 128 out of 16k mean-pooled codes favors dense, high-frequency latents (BOS, position, formatting), which matches the observation that many top features are formatting features. The most recent "SAE features as document embeddings" work max-pools ([Jiang et al., ICML 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10092v1)). HypotheSAEs selects features by L1 regression on clicks ([Movva et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04382v1)). A pooling × selection grid ({mean, max, mean-no-BOS} × {variance, L1-on-train-clicks, PCA of the full 16k code}, dropping latents with >50% density) may close part of the 22–35% gap and is cheap on cached activations. Recommender-SAE papers ([RecSAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06112v3); [arXiv:2511.18024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18024)) interpret the *recommender's* embeddings. This paper interprets the *content representation a linear policy consumes*, and the related-work section should state that distinction.

On faithfulness, the paper should say something that may sound self-defeating: because LinUCB is linear, post-hoc ablation of the learned weights is faithful by construction. The real questions are whether importance rank predicts decision change better than random, and whether correlated features compensate when the policy is retrained. The second is the ROAR question ([Hooker et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.10758v2)), and GOAR's critique that redundancy lets removal fail ([Park et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12401)) probably explains why retrain effects are weak at other layers. The fix has four parts: full deletion curves for k ∈ {1…32} comparing top-k, random-k (≥20 draws) and bottom-k; a keep-only-top-k insertion arm; paired bootstrap CIs over the 30 runs with Wilcoxon tests and Holm correction; and one redundancy-aware ablation that also removes features with |r| > 0.5. Neuronpedia's Gemma Scope 2 labels were generated on web text by models such as gemini-2.5-flash-lite ([Neuronpedia](https://www.neuronpedia.org/gemma-scope-2)). Relabeling the top 32 decision-relevant features per layer on MIND headlines, and scoring both labels with Delphi detection ([Paulo et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.13928)) and HypotheSAEs F1, turns "labels looked wrong" into "X% of Neuronpedia labels score at chance in-domain". Jiang et al. found that in-distribution relabeling improved retrieval ([arXiv:2512.10092](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10092v1)), which is the nearest published support. A policy-steering experiment (zero the weights on a sensational/clickbait concept set and measure the clicks lost against a random-feature control) is the most persuasive downstream use, but it is a week-3-or-later stretch.

## Q4: Gemma's failure replicates prior work, so the tool and the prior carry the contribution

The Q4 setup copies Krishnamurthy et al.'s best history format (summarized). Their only robust configuration was GPT-4 with suggestive framing, summarized history, reinforced CoT and **temperature 0**, and basic GPT-4 failed in >60% of replicates at T=500 ([NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.15371v3)). Gemma-3-12B's 67–70% stuck rate at T=500 and temperature 1.0 is therefore a consistency anchor, and the paper should call it one. The paper's distinctive element is the *optional* statistics tool, which tests the "tool use" intervention Krishnamurthy et al. speculated about. Reviewers will compare it with EVOLvE's always-in-prompt algorithm-guided support. That support lifted Gemini Flash few-shot win rates from 27.5% to 50.2%, while Gemma-2B/9B "mostly stay in the linear-regret regime" ([Nie et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06238v2)). The obvious reviewer question is whether the LLM adds anything over executing the tool. That makes a deterministic **argmax-of-tool-UCB policy** a mandatory baseline, along with logged agreement between the LLM's choices and argmax-UCB or argmax-P(best) on tool rounds, which mirrors Schmied et al.'s knowing-doing gap ([ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16078v1)). Schmied et al. found that forcing the model to "try all actions first" gave the largest single gain, bringing 27B close to UCB. That makes a forced round-robin start, together with temperature 0 and anonymized "Article A–J" arms, the cheapest high-value ablation set. Anonymized arms in particular separate headline-prior anchoring from exploration failure, which matters in a news paper.

The weak headline prior (Spearman 0.08–0.11, against 0.44 for the simulator's own ranking) is what the literature predicts. On Upworthy, GPT-4 zero-shot picks the winning headline 37.85% of the time against 33.02% random, and the best fine-tuned model reaches 46.86% ([LOLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02611v3)). Bayley et al. show that a systematically misaligned LLM prior can give *higher regret than cold start* ([arXiv:2604.02527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02527)). That makes **LLM-prior Thompson sampling** the cleanest new result available. Map Gemma's ranking to Beta pseudo-counts with strength n_aux ∈ {0, 10, 100, 1000}, add the LOLA-style min-of-two-UCBs variant, and contrast it with a simulator-derived prior (ρ ≈ 0.44) and a shuffled-prior control. It runs on CPU because the prior is computed once. Bayley et al. predict harm at high n_aux, and the simulator-prior contrast makes the result causal. A second model family is the main generalization request. Qwen3's thinking toggle gives a reasoning ablation with fixed weights, and Harris & Slivkins already evaluated Gemma-3 for exploitation, which provides a citation hook ([UAI 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00225v3)). Following [Kapoor et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01502v1), report a regret-vs-dollar Pareto plot and state outright that LLM+tool matches tuned UCB at roughly 10⁶–10⁷× the compute.

## Simulator credibility: claim orderings, show rank stability, and drop OPE

Standard OPE cannot be applied to MIND. The official documentation says impression orders "have been shuffled", and logging propensities are not released ([MIND docs](https://github.com/msnews/msnews.github.io/blob/master/assets/doc/introduction.md)). Any post-hoc propensity model would be unidentified. The paper should say this in one sentence and not attempt it. The credible alternative is a **simulator-family sensitivity table**. Re-run the headline comparisons under: the logistic and bilinear click models already fitted during model selection; logit temperature τ ∈ {0.5, 1.5, 2} re-anchored to 4.05% CTR; base CTR of 2% and 8%; and added logit noise or per-user random intercepts. Report Kendall τ against the base simulator, the fraction of pairwise conclusions preserved, and SIMPLER's MMRV, which weights each mis-ranked pair by its performance gap ([SIMPLER](https://simpler-env.github.io/)). Sim2Rec's ensemble-of-simulators approach shows the field accepts simulator families as a response to reality gaps ([arXiv:2305.04832](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2305.04832)). Of all the candidate experiments, this gives the most credibility per day of effort. One caution comes from project memory: the simulator configuration is frozen. The perturbations above reuse existing fits or transform logits and need no retraining. Bootstrap-refit simulators and an alternative-encoder (bge-m3) simulator are new fits and need explicit sign-off before anyone runs them.

Real-data decision-level validation is a 2027 item. Yahoo! R6A/R6B uniform-random news logs support provably unbiased replay ([Li et al., WSDM 2011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.5956)), but Webscope access is limited to academic users and approval can take weeks ([Campus Technology](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2016/01/20/yahoo-releases-machine-learning-dataset-for-academic-researchers.aspx)). The notes also recall, without verifying, that R6A has no article text, so it can check algorithm orderings but not LLM-feature claims. If R6 is wanted for 2027, the user should submit the request personally now. The Open Bandit Dataset logs uniform-random and Bernoulli-TS policies with true propensities ([Saito et al., NeurIPS 2021 D&B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.07146v5)), which allows a clean "learned simulator → policy ranking vs ground truth" check in fashion e-commerce. KuaiRand with its 2026 caption file is the only listed dataset that combines text features with random exposure ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/18159199)). Both take about a week or more and belong in the WWW/ICLR/RecSys version. The simulator's predictive numbers also need one framing correction, covered in the wording table below.

## Wording changes the notes recommend, item by item

| Topic | Current or risky framing | Recommended framing | Basis |

## Conclusion

The literature changes what the paper's contribution is. On their own, the headline numbers (a ~9x A/B gap, a 22–35% SAE penalty, a 67–70% LLM stuck rate) mostly reproduce known effects in a new domain. What is new is the *combination*: one news simulator, one set of policies, measured across the sizing, adaptivity, representation, interpretability and agentic axes, with rank stability across simulator perturbations as the evidence that the orderings are real. Recast as "which conclusions survive best-practice baselines, random controls and simulator perturbation", the paper turns several likely reviewer objections into its own results tables, and most of the work is cheap enough to finish before November 20.

Two results could overturn current claims and should be run first. The batching sweep may remove the tuned-UCB advantage. The random-SAE baseline may match trained SAEs, which would move Q3's value from "SAE features carry concepts" to "the auditing protocol works regardless of basis". Either outcome is publishable if reported honestly. Discovering one after submission is the main risk this plan avoids. For 2027, the gap reviewers cannot be talked out of is decision-level validation on real data. Since MIND cannot provide it, the OBD validation and any R6 access request (which the user must submit personally) should start right after the CALE-AI deadline.
